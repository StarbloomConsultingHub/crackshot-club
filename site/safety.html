Safety

Shot shells and crackers look harmless. Treat them like any other live ammunition. This page lists things to think about; it is not a training course or a substitute for the laws and range rules where you shoot.

Backstop and berm

Know what's behind your target and beyond it. Shoot only where a safe backstop or open, controlled ground covers every possible shot, including misses and ricochets off hard surfaces.

Shot fall

Shot fired at thrown (aerial) targets travels well past the target. Check your ammunition maker's guidance, and keep the entire downrange area clear of people, pets, houses, roads, and livestock. The club deliberately publishes no "safe distance" numbers: your ground, your ammo, your responsibility.

Eye and ear protection

Everyone on the line wears shooting glasses and hearing protection: shooters, trap pullers, and spectators. Tiny shot and cracker fragments come back.

On the line

  • Muzzle pointed in a safe direction, finger off the trigger until you're on target, action open when not shooting.
  • No one goes downrange while anyone is holding a loaded gun.
  • No alcohol or drugs on the line.
  • Lead hygiene: wash your hands after shooting, and keep food off the line (crackers you shoot at are not snacks).
  • Store and transport firearms unloaded and secured, per your state's law.

Neighbors, litter, wildlife

Talk to neighbors, keep noise to reasonable hours, and pick up hulls and target debris. Crackers break down; plastic and clays don't.

Check your local law

Discharge rules differ by state, county, and town, and many towns ban discharge inside town limits. Check with your local police or sheriff before you set up a backyard range.

Smoothbore barrel length (U.S. NFA note)

Under federal law, a shoulder-fired smoothbore firing shot shells is treated as a shotgun: the barrel must be at least 18 inches and the overall length at least 26 inches (26 U.S.C. § 5845). A rifled .22 is a rifle (16-inch barrel / 26-inch overall). Shorter guns are NFA items needing registration. States can add their own rules. The Henry Garden Gun (18.5-inch barrel, 36.5 inches overall) meets the federal minimums. The club never advises cutting, boring, or converting barrels.
