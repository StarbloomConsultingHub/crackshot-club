Gallery Games Hall / Atlas

Gallery Games Atlas

Explore the places behind American gallery games, from a Coney Island workshop to the penny arcade near Hagerstown.

Arcade context

Pen Mar’s penny arcade

Pen Mar · Maryland

Park described in a postcard record dated c. 1907

A carousel and penny arcade were among the attractions at Pen Mar Park in Washington County. The description comes from Digital Maryland’s record for a Hays Bros. postcard of nearby Buena Vista. The postcard depicts Buena Vista. A photograph, inventory or programme identifying the arcade’s games is still sought.

Sources

  • Enoch Pratt Free Library · postcard record
  • Washington County · park history

Maker

The Mangels workshop

Coney Island · New York

Documented by 1886

William F. Mangels made cast-iron gallery targets in a Coney Island machine shop. His company’s surviving Catalogue No. 4 gives a second view of the business through illustrations of the gallery scenery it offered. The catalogue entry and the biography document different points in the shop’s history.

Sources

  • Coney Island History Project · biography
  • Coney Island History Project · Catalogue No. 4

Maker

Parker’s traveling gallery

Abilene · Kansas

Late 19th century

Before C. W. Parker became known for carousels, he traveled with a portable gallery and went on to build one of his own. The Dickinson County Historical Society describes that change from operator to maker. The marker identifies his home city; the gallery itself traveled.

Sources

  • Dickinson County Historical Society · The Amusement King
  • The Strong · Parker company finding aid

Gallery

Frontierland’s original gallery

Anaheim · California

1957–1985

Disney’s archive dates the Disneyland gallery’s opening to July 12, 1957. In March 1985 the rebuilt electronic arcade took its place. The archive’s separate entries let us distinguish the original attraction from its successor.

Sources

  • Disney A to Z · original gallery
  • Disney A to Z · electronic successor

Archive

A gallery in the archive

Washington · District of Columbia

Frederick and Mary Hill Fried Folk Art Archives

The National Museum of American History’s archival description includes a file on shooting gallery targets. This marker identifies the institution that holds the research material. The surviving record gives researchers a place to follow the objects’ paper trail.

Sources

  • Smithsonian · collection record

Gallery

Magic Kingdom’s electronic arcade

Bay Lake · Florida

September 1984

Disney’s attraction history records a September 24, 1984 opening for the Florida arcade that replaced the Frontier Shooting Gallery. The California rebuild followed in 1985. The two entries preserve distinct dates for attractions often remembered under similar names.

Sources

  • Disney A to Z · attraction history
