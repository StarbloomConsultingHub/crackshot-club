# Cracker counts and shot-shell costs behind Pull the Sleeve

Research behind the club's house challenge. It replaced the old "Take the Cracker Count," which assumed a 25-cracker "sleeve." The adopted format is **Pull the Sleeve**: count the sleeve you open, shoot every cracker, and score breaks ÷ count as a percent. **The Short Stack** is the same rules on a 13-cracker Fresh Stacks-style pack. Rules and printable score cards live on the site at [/pull-the-sleeve](https://22crackshot.com/pull-the-sleeve). This note keeps the packaging and ammo evidence those rules rest on.

Prices and label counts are dated snapshots (20 November 2026, America/New_York). Snack brand names appear here only as packaging evidence; the challenge itself stays brand-neutral ("any round, scalloped, store-bought cracker").

## 1. What is actually in a sleeve

A current U.S. **retail** sleeve of original-style round butter crackers is about **30 crackers**, not 25. A **Fresh Stacks** pack is **13**. Foodservice sleeves are still sold as **32-count**. Counts can vary by a cracker or two, which is why the shooter should count the sleeve first and score as a percent.

One naming correction: the **13.7 oz box is the regular / standard box**, not the family size. Family size is 20.5–20.6 oz. Party size is 27.4 oz.

The three common retail boxes also scale cleanly: 13.7 oz × 1.5 ≈ 20.6 oz, and 13.7 oz × 2 = 27.4 oz, with 4, 6, and 8 sleeves.

### Fresh Stacks (the small packs)

Official Snackworks copy is consistent across sizes: **13 crackers per pack**.

- **11.8 oz:** 8 packs (Fairway Market and Snackworks).
- **17.8 oz family:** 12 packs (Snackworks: “12 individually wrapped cracker snack packs with 13 crackers per pack”).
- **23.7 oz party:** 16 packs (Snackworks and Fresh Grocer: 13 per stack).

Wikipedia’s 42-gram Fresh Stack of 13 crackers matches the current 16 g / 5 cracker serving (13 × 3.23 g ≈ 42 g). Treat the Wikipedia “126-gram sleeve of 32” figure as unverified; it does not match current retail serving-size math (32 × 3.2 g = 102.4 g, closer to the 3.8 oz foodservice sleeve than to 126 g).

### Variance, and why “25” was a bad foundation

People and older writeups still say **30–32**, sometimes **31–34**. A Target Q&A claims the 13.7 oz box is “4 sleeves with 32 crackers each,” but that is **128 crackers**, which does not match the current 24 × 5 = 120 label. Treat 32 as a real foodservice count and a common memory, not as today’s retail label.

**Shrinkflation, carefully:** In the **UK**, in May 2024, Mondelez confirmed to the BBC that 200 g boxes were replaced by 150 g / 140 g boxes at the same shelf price — a different market and pack, not a U.S. sleeve-count source. In the **U.S.**, I could not find a dated official announcement that retail sleeves went from 32 to 30. What we can say is: foodservice is still sold as 32-count; current U.S. retail nutrition math points to **30**; recipes and memory still say 32. Some consumer posts claim Fresh Stacks used to hold more than 13; I did not find an official prior count to confirm that. **This is exactly why a fair challenge counts the sleeve you open, instead of assuming a fixed 25, 30, or 32.**

The old 25 was a reasonable nod to a **trap singles string** (ATA and USA Clay Target League still shoot 25-target rounds), but it was incorrectly labeled “a sleeve.” The house challenge should follow the packaging. A 25-bird side event can stay in the trap tradition later if you want it; it should not be called a sleeve.

### Store-brand stand-ins

Several store brands sell a **13.7 oz** box of round butter crackers, the same weight class as the regular original box:

- Walmart **Great Value Baked Buttery Round Crackers**, 13.7 oz, serving size 5 crackers (15–16 g).
- Aldi **Savoritz Golden Round Crackers**, 13.7 oz.
- Amazon **Happy Belly** / Amazon Grocery original snack crackers, 13.7 oz.

I did not find a reliable published sleeve count for these. The challenge copy should stay “any brand of round, scalloped, store-bought crackers,” and the score card should always have a “crackers in this sleeve / pack” line. Do not use a snack trademark in the challenge name or on the card.

### Shell cost, restated (20 November 2026)

Earlier club research priced a 25-cracker string at **about $15**, from MidwayUSA’s **CCI .22 LR #12 at $11.99 per 20 ($0.60 a round)**. That Midway price is still current today (list $13.99, $11.99, in stock; Sportsman’s Warehouse also $11.99 per 20). Two important corrections:

1. **CCI #12 is sold in boxes of 20, not 50.** That is the official package quantity on [CCI’s product page](https://www.cci-ammunition.com/rimfire/cci/rimfire-shotshell/6-39.html).
2. A real retail **sleeve is about 30**, so the same Midway CCI price is **about $18 per Pull the Sleeve round**, not $15 per 25.

Winchester Super-X and Federal Game-Shok #12 are typically **50-round** boxes and are cheaper per round. Recent listings (prices move): Midway comparison pages showed Federal Game-Shok 50-round boxes on sale in the mid-teens to about $18.49 (**about $0.30–$0.37 a round**); AmmoSeek showed Federal around **$13.25 / 50 ($0.27)**; Freelands listed Winchester 50-round boxes at **$16.90 ($0.34)**. Street CCI can also come in under Midway (AmmoSeek showed about $8.44 / 20, or **$0.42 a round**).

Rough cost to a member, ammo only, crackers being cheap:

| Round | At Midway CCI ($0.60) | At typical Winchester / Federal ($0.27–$0.37) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pull the Sleeve round of 30 | ~$18 | ~$8–$11 |
| Short Stack of 13 | ~$7.80 | ~$3.50–$4.80 |

The Short Stack is the honest low-cost round. The club should not pretend a Pull the Sleeve round is a $15 / 25-shot outing if people are buying CCI 20-round boxes.

## Outcome

- Name: **Pull the Sleeve**, with **The Short Stack** as the short format (chosen by Darren, Nov 2026). Other names considered: The Full Sleeve, Sleeve Run, The Whole Sleeve, Gallery Sleeve.
- Every score card has a "crackers in this sleeve / pack" line, because counts vary by a cracker or two.
- The arcade game's sleeve was retuned from 25 to 30 and scored as a percent.
